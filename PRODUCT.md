# Product

## Platform

web

## Stack

Next.js 15 (App Router) + TypeScript, Tailwind + shadcn/ui (base-ui primitives), TanStack Virtual, Supabase (Postgres + Auth) + Drizzle ORM, deployed on Vercel. Decided by the user before this file existed, not delegated. (TanStack Table was in the original brief; v9 is an unstable pre-release, so the table uses a manual sort plus TanStack Virtual.)

## Users

Albion Online players who craft (alchemy potions, refined materials, cooking, weapons and armor, mounts) to sell for silver, checking the tool from a phone next to the game (mobile is the primary reading context) or from a desktop browser while planning a crafting session. They already know the game's mechanics; they don't need those explained, they need trustworthy numbers. Signing in with Discord is optional and only unlocks what needs a saved identity: saved plans and Discord profit alerts. Every ranking and the calculator work without an account.

## Product Purpose

Ranks crafting recipes by **realizable silver per day** (unit margin x daily sell-order volume x the player's assumed market share) instead of unit margin alone, because a high-ROI recipe that the market barely absorbs is worth less than a lower-ROI recipe with real daily volume. Success is a player trusting the ranking enough to act on it without re-deriving the math themselves.

Surfaces: five rankings (`/es/alquimia`, `/es/refinado`, `/es/cocina`, `/es/equipo`, `/es/monturas`), a calculator (`/es/calculadora`) with saved plans and a Transporte tab (weight to carry vs. bag, mount and gathering-cape capacity), a home page with the live top recipes, a methodology page and the legal pages. A saved plan is the only saved entity: one item's assumptions, listed with its profit when saved and **today** (re-priced with current market prices, hand-typed prices dropped -- the same figure its Discord alert checks), updatable in place ("Actualizar", so the alert follows it), and optionally carrying the real result of the craft (real spend + real net sale, both needed before a real profit is shown). Crafting sessions (`/es/sesiones`) were removed on 2026-09-27: they duplicated plans (item + params + frozen snapshot) with no user ever creating one; old links redirect to the plans tab.

Precision note: AODP's history endpoint (the source of every volume figure in this app) reports sell-order fulfillment, not total market activity -- `item_count` is sell-side volume, not "the whole market's" volume. Copy avoids "volumen del mercado" for exactly this reason; say "volumen de ventas" instead.

## Positioning

Three things a generic price/margin calculator cannot truthfully claim: (1) volume is inside the ranking math, not a decorative column; (2) every row shows its full derivation (price used, city, data age, what was discarded and why) so a wrong number is visibly wrong instead of silently wrong; (3) the real per-station usage-fee formula (silver per 100 nutrition, not a flat %) instead of an approximated flat crafting tax.

## Operating Context

Data comes from the free public Albion Online Data Project API (community-donated, rate-limited, attribution required) plus the official game client data dump (ao-bin-dumps): `items.json` for recipes and item values, `craftingmodifiers.xml` for city specialties, `gamedata.xml` for the crafting-quality distribution -- parsed at build time (`pnpm fetch-game-data`, fast-xml-parser), never transcribed by hand. Item weights and gathering-cape bonuses for the Transporte tool come from the same dump (`pnpm tsx scripts/fetch-transport-data.ts`, run manually when the game patches).

A GitHub Actions cron ingests hourly (`.github/workflows/ingest.yml`, single AODP server: Americas) and precomputes `market_aggregates`; the app reads only those, never raw quotes. A second cron (`daily-digest.yml`, 15:00 UTC) posts the top recipes to a Discord channel. Prices refresh hourly; 30-day volume is re-derived only when AODP publishes a new daily dump.

Supabase is on the free plan, so **egress (5 GB/month) is a first-class constraint**: the gear ranking is served from a snapshot precomputed by the ingester (`rank_snapshots`, refreshed on a new dump and every six hours), the home page reads a precomputed top list, pages revalidate hourly, and `market_aggregates` does not store empty rows (see below). Any change that widens what a page reads from the database needs to account for it.

Database access is least-privilege: `platarank_web` (Vercel, and the daily digest) can only read `recipes` and `market_aggregates` (and `rank_snapshots`); `platarank_ingest` writes them and reads plans, settings and alerts. User data (plans, settings, alerts) sits behind Supabase RLS and is reached from the browser with the user's own session. See `docs/db-least-privilege.md`.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **Recipes** (6,212): alchemy 174, refining 115, cooking 183, gear 5,711 (every weapon and armor line, including faction/hellgate lines whose rare-drop artifact materials just show "datos insuficientes" when unpriced -- the same honest-gap behavior as everything else), mounts 29.
- **City specialties are generic**: every recipe carries its own `craftingCategory` from the dump, and one lookup (`src/lib/city-specialties.ts`, from craftingmodifiers.xml) maps each category to its city and kind (crafting +15%, refining +40%). One global "ciudad donde craftea" control drives every rubro.
- **Crafting fee is the real engine formula**, confirmed by the user against the in-game Usage Fee display: `fee = ItemValue x Tax x 0.001125` (nutrition = ItemValue x 0.1125; cost = nutrition/100 x station rate), rounded like the client. `ItemValue` is the sum of every non-artifact material's `@itemvalue`, precomputed per recipe at build time (`recipe.materialItemValue`, `scripts/fetch-game-data.ts`). The station rate is an editable assumption (default 500 silver per 100 nutrition).
- **Return rate**: 18% base bonus, +15% city crafting specialty (+40% refining), +59% with focus; `return = 1 - 1/(1 + bonus)`. Artifact materials (including knowledge tomes) never get return -- a hard engine rule, not an assumption -- and neither does any material the recipe itself marks `@maxreturnamount="0"` in items.json (`noReturn` on the material, read by `fetch-game-data`): a mount's grown animal (every mount), Avalonian tokens (35 Avalonian tools), one ALCHEMY_COMMON. Until 2026-09-28 those got the return rate, which roughly doubled a focused mount's shown profit.
- **Market taxes**: 4% sale tax with premium (8% without) plus a 2.5% setup fee, in one place (`src/lib/formulas/market-tax.ts`) shared by the ranking and the calculator.
- **Gear quality is a real dimension (Q1-Q5)**: the expected sale price blends the five qualities by the game's base CraftingQualityChances (68.9/25/5/1/0.1%, fixed, not player-editable), and each quality is gated by its OWN liquidity -- volume > 0 AND at least 3 distinct days with a trade in 30 -- so a single parked sale contributes zero revenue. The editable quality-distribution control was removed at the user's explicit request: the real function that shifts these weights with focus/food/Destiny Board isn't published, and the crafting station never shows the player's real percentages, so nobody could fill it in more accurately than the default.
- **Focus cost with the player's Destiny Board** (calculator): focus per craft = `craftingFocus` x units per craft x 0.5^(FCE/10,000), rounded on the order's total. `@craftingfocus` is per unit produced, not per craft: 5 T4 poison potions (one craft) cost 5 x 84 x 0.553 = 232 in-game with a real player's board -- the golden case in `tests/destiny-focus.test.ts`. FCE = sum over the Destiny Board nodes covering the item of level x per-level bonus: mastery 30 to its category; a spec 250 to its own item plus a category share (30/22.5/15/11.25, 15 for artifact specs, ~2 tree-wide for crystal specs); refining one node per tier (250 own tier, 30 every tier of that resource). Nodes, per-level values, tier ranges and es/en/pt names come from the dump (`achievements.xml`, `localization.xml`; `pnpm tsx scripts/fetch-destiny-focus.ts` also fails if gamedata.xml's `ActionFocus costreductionconstant` stops halving every 10,000). Levels are typed once in the calculator's "Tu especialización" panel and kept in the browser's localStorage (per-viewer convenience); they only change focus figures, never silver, so plans don't store them. Mounts have no focus nodes.
- **Labourer journals (gear only, on by default)**: crafting gear fills the same-tier Blacksmith/Fletcher/Imbuer/Tinker journal, and its profit -- buy empty at the cheapest buy city, sell full at the sell cities' median listing, same sale tax as the item -- is added to cost, net revenue, profit and silver/day in the ranking, the recipe page, the calculator and alerts ("Sumar los diarios de trabajador" toggle / `diarios=0`). Fame per craft = refined resources x fame per resource (T2 1.5, T3 7.5, T4 22.5, T5 90, T6 270, T7 645, T8 1,395; x2 per enchant) x the item's `@destinyandjournalcraftfamefactor` (1.1-1.4 on artifact gear); artifacts/tokens add none, premium bonus fame never reaches journals, focus and quality don't change it. Which item fills which journal and each journal's `@maxfame` come from the dump (`src/data/generated/journals.json`, `pnpm tsx scripts/fetch-journal-data.ts`, which also fails if the T4 `@famevalue`s stop matching the table). Refining, alchemy, cooking and mounts fill no journal in the game (ORE/WOOD/... journals are gatherer journals). Open gap: full-journal sales volume doesn't cap silver/day. A saved plan without the field reads as journals ON.
- **Black Market applies to gear only** (weapons and armor). It is a structurally different venue (buy orders, quality >= requested) and is an explicit opt-in under "sell", never just another city. Other rubros never offer it. **Open gap:** the quality->=requested asymmetry is not modeled -- a crafted Q3 can also fill a lower-quality BM order and that volume isn't counted.
- **Mounts: "criar por tu cuenta"** (a toggle in the mount ranking's Filtros and an "Animal base" control in the calculator) prices the base animal as bred instead of bought grown. Horse and ox (T3-T8): the baby is a fixed NPC price (25,000 at T3, x3 per tier up to 6,075,000 at T8). Giant stag, moose, direwolf, direboar, swamp dragon, direbear and mammoth: the baby has no NPC price in the dump, so it is priced from the market like any material. Feed is the cheapest crop or meat that day (every unit of a category gives the same nutrition), times the units needed to grow it. Horse and ox give a new baby back when grown (`grownitem.offspring @chance`: 84% T3, 78.67% T4-T5, 81.04% T6, 84.2% T7, 87.36% T8), so the baby counts as price x (1 - chance) -- a T4 horse's bred cost (~22,400) lands on the grown horse's own market price (~22,600), a cross-check; the other families have 0% offspring. Growth takes 44-284 h (`@growtime`), shown next to the bred material but not folded into silver/day. Focus care (`@activefarmbonus`) raises the offspring chance further; not modeled, unverified in-game. Numbers come from `items.json`, not estimates (`src/lib/formulas/breeding.ts`). Drake (feeds on a "mount" category that isn't a market resource) and the Easter rabbit (event-only feed) are excluded: no honest cost exists for them.
- **Controls (all client-side, in the Filtros panel or the calculator)**: buy cities and sell cities separately, market share (default 10%), focus on/off, editable station rate, max data age and min volume filters, premium on/off. The ranking's state is mirrored in the URL (shareable); the calculator can also share its assumptions as a link.
- **Recipe math runs on the client on purpose** for the small stations (the server ships per-item-per-city(-per-quality) aggregates and the browser reduces them against the player's cities/params). Gear is too big for that: its default view is a server snapshot, and re-sorting or filtering it is re-ranked on the server (`rankStation`) so a truncated top 300 never hides the true top for a chosen column.
- **`market_aggregates` stores only combinations with a price, a volume, or a standing buy order.** About 70% of item x city x quality combinations are empty; the ingester prunes them and every reader treats "no row" the same as "no price". The ingester also fetches feed crops and market-traded baby animals, which are not materials of any recipe, for the breeding toggle.
- **Instant-sell price** (`buyPriceMax`): the highest standing buy order per city, tracked for real cities too (not just Black Market's own instant-sell price). A buy order escrows its silver up front, so unlike a sell listing it can never be a bait price -- no outlier trimming needed, just the highest one (`bestInstantSellPrice`). Used by the Foundry (`/artefactos`) as a "Venta instantánea" vs "Con orden de venta" toggle, defaulting to instant: artifacts rarely trade, so waiting for a player's own sell order to fill (the number every other rubro uses) is often unrealistic. Not wired into the rankings or the calculator yet -- everywhere else still assumes placing your own sell order (`netSellMultiplier`'s "assumes a premium crafter placing their own sell order").
- **Raw price/volume history is NOT persisted** (a deliberate deviation from the original brief): a raw insert per hourly run would fill Supabase's free tier in hours, and nothing queries it. AODP's own history endpoint keeps the same 30-day window.
- **30-day volume comes from AODP's daily dump**, not REST `/stats/history`: the ingester finds the latest `db_backup_*.tgz`, streams it (gunzip + tar-stream + a tuple regex over `market_history`, never held in memory) and only re-parses it when the URL changed (`ingest_state` stores the last one); the other hourly runs only refresh prices. Two gotchas verified against a live dump and live `/stats/history`, undocumented publicly: (1) `market_history.location` is the trading-post sub-zone id (`"<City> Market"`), not the open-world cluster id, and Black Market uses Caerleon's open-world id (3003); (2) since 2026-09-09 the table carries two overlapping series per day (`aggregation=1` hourly and `aggregation=6`, six-hourly) -- summing both doubles recent volume, so exactly one series is picked per day.
- **Price hygiene**: at ingest a sell price of 100,000 or more above 20x the median of the item's other quotes is dropped; at compute time, with 3+ quotes, anything below 0.4x or above 2.5x the median is trimmed, and a price far from its own 30-day average is discarded and named (`outlier_self`, `src/lib/formulas/outliers.ts`). Every discard shows in the row's derivation.
- **The AODP client throttles with a real sliding window** covering both caps (180 requests/min and 300 per 5 min).
- **Discord alerts** re-price a saved plan after every ingest and post to the user's own webhook only when profit crosses the threshold from below (no repeats while it stays above; webhook errors are surfaced to the user; `allowed_mentions` is empty so plan names can't ping anyone).
- Every ranked row must expose the silver-per-day figure and an expandable derivation (price source + city + age, materials + costs with the cheapest city to buy each in, station fee, return rate, market share, and every discarded outlier with its reason). The 0-100 data-quality score that once sat beside each row was removed at the user's explicit request; the derivation's own fields already carry that signal.
- Data can be missing or stale per city (Brecilien especially); the UI says so explicitly rather than hiding it or fabricating a number.
- SEO and AEO: `/` redirects to `/es`; every route has its own metadata and canonical (`pageMetadata`, `src/lib/seo.ts`); JSON-LD and per-station FAQ copy (`src/components/json-ld.tsx`, `src/lib/station-seo.ts`); an indexable page per recipe (`/es/receta/[itemId]`); an about page (`/es/acerca`); generated `sitemap.xml`, `robots.txt`, `manifest` and `llms.txt` for LLM crawlers. `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL` is the single source of the absolute domain.
- Security headers (`next.config.ts`), GitHub workflows with minimal permissions and pinned actions.

**Open gaps (tracked, not silent):**
- No golden-case captures from the live game yet; the fee/return formulas are verified by tests and user confirmation, not by recorded in-game screens (`fixtures/golden-cases.json`, one `todo` test).
- The quality-reroll matrix (`RerollQualityChances` in gamedata.xml) is parsed by nothing; "craft Q3, then reroll" is uncalculated (its silver cost would have to be user-entered).
- The ingester still refreshes every item hourly at a single priority; the brief asked for low-volume items to refresh less often.
- The calculator doesn't model daily bonuses. Rankings don't sort by silver per focus point yet (the calculator already knows the player's focus cost).
- Black Market quality asymmetry (above).
- Only the Americas server has data; the server selector lists Europe and Asia as "sin datos todavía".

## Brand Commitments

Name: PlataRank (repo/package `platarank`). Identity is the wax-seal mark plus the "guild ledger" chrome documented in DESIGN.md.

## Evidence on Hand

Real market data is live: the hourly ingester runs against AODP Americas and `market_aggregates` holds about 70,000 non-empty item-city-quality rows (as of 2026-09-27, after pruning empty ones from 240,064) for ~7,000 priced items (gear priced across Q1-Q5). Recipe data (6,212 recipes) is real, pulled from the official ao-bin-dumps client data.

## Product Principles

- The ranking metric is silver/day, always -- never let a UI element imply unit margin is the primary signal.
- Never show a number without a way to see where it came from; "trust the derivation" is the product's entire pitch.
- Silence is worse than an ugly caveat: missing/thin data gets flagged in the row, never smoothed over.
- Never fabricate game data: numbers come from the client dump or a user-confirmed in-game check, and anything we can't source (the quality function, Drake feed, event animals) is left out and documented instead of estimated.
- Mobile is not an afterthought breakpoint -- it's the primary reading context, checked one-handed next to the game.
- Simple over clever: every assumption (market share, station rate, focus, which cities count, breeding your own mounts) is a visible, adjustable control, never a hidden default the user has to reverse-engineer.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Mobile-first operating context, plus the baseline the accessibility audit established: `prefers-reduced-motion` removes movement but keeps color feedback, a skip-to-content link on every page, text at 12px or larger (one small city-bonus tag is 11px), touch targets of 44px on the mobile surfaces and at least 24px for small text links, tooltips that open on tap/click/Enter (never `title`), polite live regions that stay mounted, stale/illiquid data marked by a dashed underline as well as opacity (readable in grayscale), retryable error states, and dark-only by decision. Copy is Spanish (rioplatense, with tildes), with English and Brazilian Portuguese translations (`/en`, `/pt`).
